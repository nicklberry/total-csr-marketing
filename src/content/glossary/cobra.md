					<h2>What Is COBRA in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p><b>For clients:</b> In simple terms, COBRA is a federal law that lets you, as a former employee, continue your health insurance for a period after your employment ends. </p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> COBRA (Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act) is a federal statute that mandates most group health plans provide a cobra continuation coverage option for eligible employees and their families upon the occurrence of specific qualifying life events.</p><p>These events typically include job loss, hour reduction, transition between jobs, death, divorce, and other life-changing events. COBRA coverage can last from 18 to 36 months, depending on the nature of the qualifying event. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About COBRA? </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What happens when COBRA coverage ends?</b> </p><p>When COBRA coverage ends, individuals must seek alternative health insurance. This could be through a new employer’s group health plan, a spouse’s insurance, private sector businesses, or federal programs like Medicare. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What are the COBRA payment responsibilities for employers?</b> </p><p>Employers typically don't have a COBRA payment responsibility. The qualified beneficiaries bear the complete cost of COBRA coverage, which includes the full premium and a potential 2% administrative fee. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Are there alternatives to COBRA?</b> </p><p>Yes, qualifying individuals could look at health coverage alternatives like short term health insurance, spouse's health plan, Medicaid, or private insurance. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What are the consequences of not complying with COBRA rules?</b> </p><p>Failure to adhere to COBRA's stringent requirements can lead to substantial statutory penalties, legal fees, and damage claims. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain COBRA to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Personal Lines client:</b> Think of COBRA like a bridging program. It maintains your health insurance coverage after job loss or reduction in working hours till you find another solution. <br /></p><p><b>Small Business Owner:</b> As a business owner, you play an instrumental role in informing departing employees about their rights to continue health benefits under COBRA. This helps them maintain health insurance and can really make a difference during difficult transitions. </p><p><b>CFO or Risk Manager:</b> The crux of COBRA mandates that eligible employees should have the ability to retain their group healthcare benefits under certain scenarios. It's important to follow COBRA rules to avoid possible legal penalties and lawsuits. </p>
