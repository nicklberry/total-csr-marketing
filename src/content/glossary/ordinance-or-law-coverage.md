<h2>Ordinance or Law Coverage – A Crucial Insurance for Reconstruction Costs </h2>				
		<p>What would happen if a fire caused significant damage to your commercial property or home? You'd think your insurance would cover the rebuild, but what about meeting new building codes or constructing handicap accessibility that wasn't there before? </p><p>When law coverage comes into play, it can significantly impact how your insurance claim is handled. It can be the difference between having sufficient coverage to rebuild your property entirely or not. </p>

					<h2>What Is Ordinance or Law Coverage in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>For a client, ordinance or law coverage insurance helps pay for costs associated with updating your building to meet local building codes and regulations during rebuilding after a covered loss.  </p><p>For an insurance professional, the term is a type of additional coverage that insures against increased construction costs due to changes, updates or enforcement in local building ordinances. This often appears as an endorsement on a homeowners insurance policy or commercial property insurance policy and is usually not included in basic coverage limits. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Ordinance or Law Coverage </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What is Ordinance or Law Coverage?</b> </p><p>Ordinance or Law Coverage is a type of insurance policy that offers financial protection against the increased costs of construction, following a covered loss, to comply with current building codes and regulations. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How Does Ordinance or Law Insurance Impact Older Buildings?</b> </p><p>Older structures often don’t meet current building codes. So if an older home or commercial property is damaged, and needs repairs or rebuilding, updated requirements can add to reconstruction costs. Also, if damaged, and un-upgraded, structural components (like clay pipes) need replacement, ordinance or law coverage would cover those unforeseen costs. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What's the Difference between Partial Loss and Total Loss in this Context?</b> </p><p>If a property experiences a partial loss, ordinance or law coverage could pay to upgrade the damaged portion and possibly the undamaged portion if local authorities require it. In case of a total loss, this coverage will fund the extra cost of building a new structure that meets the latest standards. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Can Building Code Upgrade Coverage Cover Energy Efficiency and Handicap Accessibility Upgrades?</b> </p><p>In recent years, local authorities have incorporated energy efficiency and handicap accessibility requirements into local building codes, especially for commercial properties. These could be covered if improvements are necessary due to a covered loss and required by local building codes. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Ordinance or Law Coverage </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A fire engulfed an older home, leading to significant destruction. The homeowners had sufficient insurance coverage for the dwelling, but during the rebuilding process, the local building inspection discovered that the electrical wiring did not meet the current building codes. The required electrical system upgrade caused unforeseen costs that their ordinance or law coverage helped cover.  </p><p><b>Scenario 2: </b>A restaurant suffered severe water damage when the pipes burst. To reopen the restaurant, local authorities mandated expanded handicap accessibility in the bathroom. Without the ordinance or law coverage, the restaurant owner would have needed to fund these additional expenses out-of-pocket. <br /></p><p><b>Scenario 3: </b>A homeowner's roof was critically damaged during a storm, necessitating a complete replacement. The current building codes required the inclusion of a more advanced and expensive roofing material to enhance energy efficiency. Thanks to their ordinance or law coverage, the homeowner was not burdened with the additional cost. </p>
