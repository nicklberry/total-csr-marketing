<h2>Investigation – A Process of Assessing Insurance Claims </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> An investigation is like solving a puzzle. It's when insurance companies look closely at all the details of an incident to decide whether to pay a claim or not. </p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> In insurance, an investigation is a systematic, detailed examination conducted by insurers or their representatives—often insurance adjusters—to validate a claim's legitimacy, determine liability, and calculate the amount of loss. The investigation commences upon a claim's filing and can involve a variety of techniques, such as reviewing policy documents, inspecting physical damages, interviewing witnesses, and evaluating medical reports. </p><p>Imagine your client calls with a serious auto accident claim. Before they can expect any payout, their insurance company will conduct an investigation to assess the damage, liability, and potential fraud. Understanding this process can help you guide your clients and manage their expectations better. </p>

					<h2>What Is Investigation in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>In the realm of insurance, an investigation is not akin to a detective's work in a criminal investigation or similar to the task of the bureau of investigation. Even though it doesn't involve chasing criminals like in a crime show, it's essential for any claim process. From a minor traffic collision to a substantial airplane crash, almost every claim lodged by a policyholder will go through an investigation.  </p><p>Insurers use investigations to validate whether a claim is valid and to calculate the claim's value. For instance, in a fire investigation process, the investigators may review the evidences to determine the cause of the fire— was it accidental or intentionally set? Interviews with policyholders, witnesses, and sometimes professionals like law enforcement or private investigators are parts of the elaborate investigation procedure. </p><p>Documents are also critically reviewed during an investigation. These can come in various forms like police reports in the case of an auto accident, medical reports for injuries, or even geospatial data and remote sensing for assessing property damages from natural disasters. More complex claims might require an extensive insurance adjuster-led investigation team, potentially involving forensic science investigation or site survey. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Investigation </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>How long does an investigation take?</b> </p><p>The duration of an investigation generally depends on the complexity of the claim and the amount of data involved. For example, in highly complex cases involving severe damages, insurers might carry out a thorough forensic science investigation or site survey, which can extend the investigation timeline. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What happens if fraud is detected during an investigation?</b> </p><p>If an investigation uncovers fraudulent activities, such as discrepancies in the claimant's account of events or mismatches in physical evidence, claim denial is a common outcome. In more severe instances, legal actions can ensue, including charges of insurance fraud. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How can investigators collect data about an event?</b> </p><p>Data for investigations can come from various sources. It could include on-site inspections, interviews with the claimant, witnesses and law enforcement, and document reviews. Some complex investigations might require the use of specialized techniques like geospatial data analysis or remote sensing. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Can a policyholder refuse to participate in an investigation?</b> </p><p>Generally, policyholders are expected to cooperate with the insurance company's investigation. Non-cooperation can lead to complications in claim processing, including delays or potential denial of the claim. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Investigation </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A client submitted a claim for water damage at their home. During an investigation, it was revealed that the customer had neglected to maintain their plumbing system, leading to the damage. The claim was subsequently denied because the damage was a result of negligence. <br /></p><p><b>Scenario 2: </b>After a severe storm, a commercial property owner made a claim for roof damage. The adjuster, using remote sensing and geospatial data during the investigation, verified that storm was intense enough to cause the reported damage. The claim was approved, and the business could quickly repair the damages. </p><p><b>Scenario 3: </b>A client was involved in a car accident and filed a claim for the medical expenses. However, the investigation revealed discrepancies between the client's account of the severity of injuries and the medical reports. This raised red-flags, opening an additional investigation for potential fraud. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Investigation to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Personal Lines client:</b> "Think of the investigation as a way to ensure you get a fair settlement. The insurance company checks all the details of your claim, kind of like assembling puzzle pieces, to determine how much they should pay you." </p><p><b>Small Business owner:</b> "During the investigation process, the insurance company will assess all elements of your claim, similar to a detailed audit. This helps them confirm the event, determine who's responsible, and figure out how much your claim is worth." <br /></p><p><b>CFO or Risk Manager:</b> "The investigation serves to protect both your organization and the insurer. The insurer will evaluate your claim thoroughly, just like your internal risk assessments. This ensures that all claim payouts accurately reflect the loss and help maintain the policy's integrity." </p>
