<h2>What Is Pure Risk in Insurance?</h2>
<p>To a client, pure risk might sound like an event that could result in a loss and nothing else. There's no chance of making a profit or experiencing any gain. </p>
<p>In technical terms, pure risk is a situation characterized by exposure to harm or loss only. It typically appears on insurance policies, where the risk insured is a situation presenting a binary outcome: either a definite loss occurs or not. It's commonly covered in most standard insurance policies as insurable risk, and most natural disasters provide typical examples. </p>

<h2>Common Questions About Pure Risk </h2>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What are examples of pure risk in the real world?</b> </p>
<p>A few examples of pure risk include natural disasters such as hurricanes or earthquakes, fires, accidents causing injury, theft from a home or business, and legal liabilities. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>Can pure risk be insured?</b> </p>
<p>Yes. The core concept of risk insurance is to protect against pure risk. When an insurance company insures a pure risk, it agrees to cover the financial loss of the policyholder if a definite loss occurs. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What is the difference between pure risk and speculative risk?</b> </p>
<p>Pure risk is characterized by a binary outcome – loss occurs or it does not. Speculative risk, however, presents three outcomes – a loss, a gain, or no change. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>Can pure risk be managed?</b> </p>
<p>Absolutely. Through proper risk management techniques such as risk transfer, or purchasing insurance coverage, individuals and businesses can manage their exposure to pure risk. </p>

<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Pure Risk </h2>
<p><b>Scenario 1:</b> A homeowner in a flood-prone area fails to buy adequate flood insurance. A severe flood occurs, causing substantial property damage. Because of the oversight in assessing their pure risk exposure, the homeowner now faces a significant financial loss. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 2:</b> A business owner faces a lawsuit following a customer's slip and fall incident within their premises. Although a common liability pure risk, the business owner underestimated its potential impact and did not have adequate liability coverage, leading to a significant payout from their personal assets. </p>

<h2>How to Explain Pure Risk to Clients </h2>
<p>For personal lines: "Think of it like this. Pure risk is an event where the only possible outcome is a loss. Like if lightning hits your house and starts a fire - that's a pure risk." </p>
<p>For small businesses: "Pure risk is a situation where the only possibility is a loss or nothing at all. No chance for gain. Natural disasters, legal liabilities, or loss from theft are such examples." </p>
<p>For CFO/risk manager: "Pure risk in insurance refers to conditions presenting a binary outcome—loss occurs or not. It’s crucial for financial risk management to identify potential pure risks and take necessary steps to insure them." </p>
