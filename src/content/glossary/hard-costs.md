<h2>Hard Costs – Construction Expenses That Are Tangible </h2>
<p><b>In plain language:</b> Hard costs are physical and tangible expenses related to a construction project—like the cost of materials or labor. </p>
<p><b>Technical definition:</b> Hard costs — often referred to as "brick and mortar costs" — encompass all physical labor and material expenses tied directly to the construction of a structure. Hard costs can include site work, building structure, utilities, landscaping, and contractor fees. These are distinguished from soft costs, which cover things like architectural design fees or permits. </p>
<p>Imagine constructing a new building and focusing only on the tangible materials and labor, overstepping all other expenses—like design, permits, and legal fees. These ignored expenses, known as 'soft costs', can ramp up your project budget unexpectedly. </p>

<h2>What Are Hard Costs in Construction? </h2>
<p>In the landscape of a construction project, hard costs play a pivotal role. They form a significant part of the construction budget, encompassing all materials, labor, and any other tangible expense linked to the actual construction. </p>
<p>On a project's budget or on the declarations page of an insurance policy relevant to the construction industry, you'll often see hard costs itemized along with soft costs. It's crucial for professionals to distinguish between these terms as they have explicit impacts on the completion and overall expenses of a project. </p>
<p>Reason for existence? It's simple. Hard costs provide a concrete figure—helping both the client and the insurer understand the tangible cost of a construction project. </p>

<h2>Common Questions About Hard Costs </h2>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What is the difference between hard and soft costs in construction?</b> </p>
<p>Although both are key components in construction costs, hard costs refer to the tangible aspects of a construction project like the materials and labor, while soft costs encompass the intangible expenses, such as permits, architectural services, and legal fees. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>How does a misunderstanding of hard costs affect a construction project?</b> </p>
<p>Misunderstanding or underestimating hard costs can lead to cost overruns or project delays. A project budget needs an accurate estimation of these costs for materials and labor, and understand the implications on the project timeline. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>Why are hard costs important in insurance for construction projects?</b> </p>
<p>Hard costs provide a baseline for determining the extent of coverage required, impacting the premium. Simply, they help provide a tangible value to the structure and guide underwriting decisions. </p>

<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Hard Costs </h2>
<p><b>Scenario 1:</b>  Client constructing a commercial building miscalculated their hard costs by underestimating the cost of materials. Consequently, they ran out of budget before completing the building. The construction project ran over budget due to incomplete accounting of hard costs. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 2:</b> In another construction project, the client did not include hard costs like site work or landscaping in their initial budget. When it was time to carry out these tasks, they were unprepared for the additional expenditure. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 3:</b>  A construction project was put on hold as the clients underestimated their hard costs, particularly labor, resulting in insufficient funds to pay their workers. This delay meant higher soft costs due to extended project management and legal fees. </p>

<h2>How to Explain Hard Costs to Clients </h2>
<p><b>Personal Lines client</b> "Think of hard costs as the physical stuff you need for construction—the bricks, cement, the workers who lay them—it's all part of your hard costs." </p>
<p><b>Small Business owner</b> "Your hard costs include anything tangible, like the materials for your new office space, your construction crew's wages, and even the utilities during construction." </p>
<p><b>CFO or Risk Manager</b> "Your construction project's hard costs would cover every physical, direct expense towards the construction—materials, labor, site work, etc. It forms a significant part of your construction budget and directly impacts your bottom line." </p>
