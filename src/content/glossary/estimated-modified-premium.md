<h2>Estimated Modified Premium – The Projected Workers Comp Premium </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> The estimated modified premium is a projection of your workers' compensation insurance premium for the policy term. It takes into account your past claims history, business size, and the riskiness of your industry. <br /></p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> The estimated modified premium is a forecast of your workers' compensation premium that reflects your experience modification rate or mod factor. It typically appears on your workers' compensation policy's declaration page and is calculated by multiplying the standard premium by your experience modifier. </p><p>Understanding what drives up your workers' comp premium can be a head-scratcher, especially when unfamiliar terms like estimated modified premium and experience mod come into play. Let's break it down to its nuts and bolts. </p>

					<h2>What Is Estimated Modified Premium in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>The estimated modified premium is an integral part of the workers' compensation insurance equation. It's more than just a projection of your premium costs—it reflects your company's safety record compared to other businesses in your industry. <br /></p><p>This term appears on the declarations page of your workers' compensation policy. The estimated modified premium factors in the experience modification rate (EMR), otherwise known as the experience mod, mod factor, or ex-mod. </p><p>An experience mod is a critical element in the calculation of your workers' comp premium. It reflects your company’s less or more than average industry risk as determined by past claims and potential future risks. A lower experience mod signifies less risk, and thus, a lower premium.</p><p>The estimated modified premium holds an important place within broader coverage concepts as an incentive for risk management, encouraging businesses to maintain a safe work environment to lower insurance costs. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Estimated Modified Premium </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>How is the estimated modified premium calculated?</b> </p><p>The estimated modified premium is calculated by multiplying your standard premium by your experience modifier. The higher the experience rating modification, the higher your estimated modified premium. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What affects my experience mod?</b> </p><p>Past claims, the severity of those claims, and your payroll are what primarily determine your experience mod. Improving workplace safety and thereby reducing claims can lower your experience mod, which, in turn, reduces your estimated modified premium. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How does experience rating factor into the premium?</b> </p><p>Experience rating is a way to tailor the insurance premium to a business by considering the company’s past performance. A lower experience rating equals lower mod factor and lower estimated modified premium. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Does my estimated modified premium remain constant?</b> </p><p>No, it can change. If your experience modification rate increases or decreases, your estimated premium may also change. Any changes in the experience mod calculation can directly impact the premium. </p>
