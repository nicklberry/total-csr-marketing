<h2>Waiver of Subrogation – A Contractual Provision Limiting Subrogation Rights</h2>				
		<p>Picture this: A grease fire breaks out in a food truck while it's in service. It causes property damage to a lighting fixture company's product. The lighting fixture owner's insurance company pays out the claim and under ordinary circumstances, may have rights to recover the claim amount from the food truck owner's insurer through subrogation. However, a waiver of subrogation clause in the lease agreement with the food truck prevents that recovery action, keeping the peace among all parties involved.</p>		
					<h2>TL;DR </h2>				
		<ul><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>What a waiver of subrogation is in one simple sentence:</b> A waiver of subrogation is a contractual provision where one party agrees to limit the rights of their own insurance company in recovering the claim amount paid on a loss from a negligent third party.</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Why it matters in day-to-day agency work:</b> It helps maintain business relationship between parties in an agreement, even after a loss occurs and a claim is paid.</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>One common pitfall or misunderstanding:</b> Assuming that a waiver of subrogation is automatically included in an insurance policy.</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>One quick win or best practice for agencies:</b> Review insurance policies and contracts to ensure the waiver of subrogation is included if required by agreements.</li></ul>

					<h2>Common Questions About Waiver of Subrogation </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What Is a Subrogation Waiver?</b> </p><p>It's a clause in an insurance contract that limits the insurance company's right to seek reimbursement from a party that caused a loss. It often applies in situations where two parties have a business relationship they want to maintain, such as between landlords and tenants. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Why Do I Need a Waiver of Subrogation?</b> </p><p>If you're entering into a contract, the other party may require a waiver of subrogation to limit potential litigation costs associated with subrogation claims. It can make resolution of claims faster and preserve your business relationship. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What Does Waiver of Subrogation Endorsement Mean?</b> </p><p>An endorsement adds or modifies provisions in the original policy. In this case, the waiver of subrogation endorsement limits the insurer's subrogation rights, often for an additional fee. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Can There be a Waiver of Subrogation in Auto Insurance Policies?</b> </p><p>Yes. For example, in work comp insurance, it can affect the rights of the insurance company to recover amounts paid on behalf of the injured employee from the at-fault party. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Waiver of Subrogation to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>To a Personal Lines Client:</b> It's like a pre-agreement between the insurance companies involved that they won't go after each other for money if a claim happens. It's designed to keep everything flowing smoothly. </p><p><b>To a Small Business Owner:</b> This waiver can be crucial in preserving a working relationship after a claim occurs. It prevents your insurance company from seeking compensation from any other parties after an incident. </p><p><b>To a CFO/Risk Manager:</b> This clause helps limit potential litigation costs and complications by preventing the insurer from pursuing subrogation, fostering smoother claims processing. However, the endorsement might result in higher insurance premiums, so it's important to evaluate the cost-benefit scenario. </p>
