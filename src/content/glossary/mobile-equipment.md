<h2>Mobile Equipment – Vehicles Not Designed for Public Roads </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> Mobile equipment refers to machinery that is typically not designed for use on public roads. Often, it's large equipment used in construction, farming, or similar industries. Examples might include forklifts, backhoes, or cranes. <br /></p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> Mobile equipment in the insurance context typically appears in commercial auto and general liability policies. It broadly encompasses machinery or devices that aren't subject to motor vehicle registration laws, including vehicles primarily designed for use off-public roads, or those maintained for use solely on the insured's premises. This can span across self-propelled equipment, track-mounted equipment, or rail-mounted mobile equipment. Distinctions are often made for permanently attached equipment and skid-mounted equipment. </p><p>Imagine the costly damage if a construction crane fell onto adjacent property. Mobile equipment coverage becomes critical here. </p>

					<h2>What Is Mobile Equipment in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>Mobile equipment in insurance is a classification used in commercial auto and liability policies. By establishing the type of equipment, insurers can determine if a loss is included within the policy's scope of protection. <br /></p><p>Equipment not designed or licensed for road use often falls into mobile equipment classification, including machinery like forklifts or drilling rigs.  </p><p>This term typically appears in policy forms and endorsements. It is crucial as it differentiates vehicles covered under a commercial auto policy from those covered under general liability. For instance, a forklift used on a premise will be regarded as mobile equipment under liability policy, but once it crosses the road, it might be looked at as an auto under a commercial vehicle policy. Hence, understanding mobile equipment can save agencies and businesses from substantial losses. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Mobile Equipment </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>How are mobile equipment accidents covered under insurance?</b> </p><p>Mobile equipment accidents on job sites fall under a general liability policy. But if the equipment crosses onto a public road, it could potentially be deemed a vehicle and fall under a commercial auto policy. This can impact where claims are filed and the compensation received. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Does mobile equipment need a separate insurance policy?</b> </p><p>Often, no. However, depending on the type of equipment and its use, agencies might recommend equipment floater insurance which covers the value of the equipment itself if it's damaged or stolen. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How does mobile equipment safety impact my insurance premiums?</b> </p><p>Following best practices for mobile equipment safety can translate to lower insurance premiums. An effective equipment safety program can reduce the incident of accidents, leading to fewer claims and thus, lower premiums. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Why is understanding mobile equipment important?</b> </p><p>Understanding mobile equipment is vital to ensuring appropriate coverage and preventing unpaid claims. If equipment is wrongly categorized, it may lead to coverage gaps and potential losses. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Mobile Equipment </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1:</b> ABC Construction owned a backhoe used for off-road projects. When the backhoe accidentally damaged a nearby building, ABC's general liability policy covered the damages because the backhoe was deemed mobile equipment. <br /></p><p><b>Scenario 2:</b> XYZ Corporation owned a forklift used in their operation yard. However, when taking it across the road, the forklift hit a car. XYZ's claim was denied because their liability policy considered the forklift a vehicle when used on public roads. </p><p><b>Scenario 3:</b> Smith's Landscaping owned a fleet of mowers. When one caught fire, their commercial policy didn't cover the damages. If they'd classified the mower as mobile equipment, their loss would've been covered. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Mobile Equipment to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Personal Lines client:</b> Think of mobile equipment as big machines that aren't meant for public roads, like forklifts or cranes, and usually covered by your business liability policy. <br /></p><p><b>Small Business owner:</b> For your business, mobile equipment refers to machinery like cranes or backhoes which aren't meant for road use. It's crucial to classify your equipment correctly to avoid any unexpected coverage gaps. </p><p><b>CFO or Risk Manager:</b> Mobile equipment is a classification for machinery or tools not licensed for road use. They're generally protected under a general liability policy. However, once they're used on public roads, they could potentially be considered vehicles, potentially complicating claims and creating coverage gaps. </p>
