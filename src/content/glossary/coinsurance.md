					<h2>Common Questions About Coinsurance </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What does coinsurance mean in a property insurance policy?</b> </p><p>Coinsurance in a property insurance policy is a clause that requires the insured to have insurance covering a specified percentage of the property's value. If the property is not insured to the prescribed value, there is a coinsurance penalty in the event of a loss. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Does coinsurance apply to every kind of insurance?</b> </p><p>No, coinsurance is not applicable to all forms of insurance. For instance, it is common in health and property insurance but less common in auto insurance. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What’s the purpose of coinsurance in a policy?</b> </p><p>The coinsurance clause encourages policyholders to carry insurance equal to a specified percentage of the total value of property to receive full benefits in the event of a loss. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How does coinsurance impact my claims?</b> </p><p>If your insurance coverage doesn’t meet or exceed the coinsurance requirement, it could result in a reduced claim payout. This is known as a coinsurance penalty. </p>		
					<h2>Coinsurance vs. Deductible</h2>				
		<p>Coinsurance and deductibles are both methods of cost sharing in an insurance policy, but they work differently. A deductible is a set amount the insured must pay before the insurer starts to pay, while coinsurance is a percentage the insured pays after the deductible is met. <br /> </p><table><tbody><tr><td><p> </p></td><td><p>Deductible </p></td><td><p>Coinsurance </p><p>  </p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Primary use case </p></td><td><p>To limit the insurer’s exposure to small claims. </p></td><td><p>To motivate the insured to carry adequate insurance and share in the risk of loss. </p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Concept type </p></td><td><p>Fixed amount the insured pays before the insurer pays. </p></td><td><p>Percentage of cost the insured pays after the deductible. </p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Typical exceptions </p></td><td><p>Applies to each loss or each policy year (for health insurance). </p></td><td><p>Does not apply until after the deductible is met. </p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Who is most affected by errors </p></td><td><p>Individuals and businesses who don’t understand how their policy works. </p></td><td><p>Insureds who are underinsured and face a coinsurance penalty as a result. </p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Common mistakes </p></td><td><p>Selecting too high of a deductible and not being able to afford it at the time of a claim. </p></td><td><p>Not understanding the implications of coinsurance and underinsuring property. </p></td></tr></tbody></table>
