<h2>Products - Commodities or Services Sold to Customers </h2>
<p><b>In plain language:</b> In the world of business, products are any items or services that a company sells to their customers. Think about anything from a soft drink you purchase at a store to an online subscription to a streaming service - these are all examples of products. </p>
<p><b>Technical definition:</b> Within the context of the insurance industry, "products" are any tangible or intangible goods, services, or digital entities that are marketed and sold by a business entity to its target market. Products are often central to liability coverages and can form the basis for claims arising from sold goods or products liability claims. </p>
<p>If you imagine a store filled with different types of products, you probably envision items like electronics, clothing, food, beverages, or even furniture. But in the business world, the definition of products is broad and can include anything a company offers for sale to customers. </p>

<h2>What Is Products in Insurance? </h2>
<p>A "product" in the insurance industry encapsulates a broad range of items — from physical commodities like groceries produce, clothing, or appliances, to intangible offerings like software, music, eBooks, or services. These elements are what a business offers for sale, what generates their revenue, and ultimately what defines their operation. </p>
<p>In the insurance world, understanding the type and nature of products a business sells is particularly important as it directly impacts the types and levels of risk that the business might face. For instance, a software company offering digital products may face different risks and need various coverage compared to a manufacturer producing industrial machinery. </p>
<p>Moreover, products play a key role in policies that deal with liability, such as Products Liability coverage, which protects a business in the event their product causes harm or damage. </p>

<h2>Common Questions About Products </h2>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What is the product?</b> </p>
<p>The product refers to any item or service that a company has made available for sale to its customers. This could range from physical goods like a book, to digital entities like a membership plan for a streaming service, or even a financial service such as a loan. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What are examples of products?</b> </p>
<p>Examples of products are virtually limitless as they span across several industries and domains. For instance, a bakery's products might include bread, pastries, and cakes, while a tech startup's product could be a mobile app or a software solution. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>Why is it necessary to categorize products?</b> </p>
<p>Categorizing products helps agencies understand the risks involved, which is critical for determining which types of insurance coverage a business needs and the rate of premiums. For instance, a company that produces medical devices might require a different level of insurance coverage compared to a firm that sells second-hand clothes. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What are some other product-related terms that agencies need to know?</b> </p>
<p>Aside from the basic products terminology, insurance professionals should also be familiar with terms like 'product liability', 'product recall', and 'product warranty'. These concepts often come into play when dealing with claims related to the products that a business sells. </p>

<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Products </h2>
<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A furniture company sold a chair that was found to have been made with a weak structure leading to an injury. The company faced a products liability claim, leading to financial losses because they did not have sufficient coverage for their products. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 2: </b>A beverage company faced a recall of a soft drink product from the market due to contamination. The loss was on a multi-million scale, leading to significant financial damage. This was covered by their product liability insurance. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 3: </b>A software firm was sued for a glitch in their digital products that led to a loss of data for their clients. The firm's product liability insurance provided coverage for the legal defence costs and damages claimed.</p>

<h2>How to Explain Products to Clients </h2>
<p><b>Personal Lines client </b>Think of products as anything you purchase from a store or online, like a shirt, a refrigerator, or a Netflix subscription. </p>
<p><b>Small Business owner </b>From the perspective of your business, products are whatever you sell to your customers. This could be a physical product like a book, a digital product like software, or a service like home cleaning. </p>
<p><b>CFO or Risk Manager </b>In the context of insurance, products are critical in determining the kinds of liability your company could face. It's vital to correctly identify and classify all the products your company offers when setting up coverage plans. </p>
