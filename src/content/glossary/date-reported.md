<h2>Date Reported – Why Timing Matters on Claims </h2>
<p><b>In plain language:</b> Date reported is the specific day when a loss or incident is first reported to an insurance carrier. This date is very important in determining if a claim is covered, especially in claims-made policies. </p>
<p><b>Technical definition:</b> Date reported is the day a claim or incident is first reported to the insurance company or carrier. It is a crucial element in determining coverage under a claims-made policy, which only covers claims reported during the policy period or an extended reporting period. It is typically mentioned in the claims conditions and provisions of an insurance policy. </p>
<p>Have you ever had a water damage claim denied because it was reported too late? That's just one example of how pivotal the date reported can be in determining insurance coverage.</p>

<h2>What Is the Date Reported in Insurance? </h2>
<p>Date reported is a term that refers to the specific date when a claim or incident is first reported to an insurance company. This date can be crucial in determining whether a claim is covered, especially under claims-made policies and claims-made and reported policies. </p>
<p>In claims-made policies, coverage depends on both the date of the loss and the date the claim is reported. This policy only covers claims that are made and reported during the policy period or any applicable extended reporting period (ERP). </p>
<p>Distinct from occurrence form policies that cover claims arising from incidents during the coverage period, regardless of when they are reported, the claims-made form is more time-sensitive. </p>
<p>The date reported has broader coverage implications too: it can influence the application of deductibles, impact the statute of limitations, and affect coinsurance calculations. </p>

<h2>Common Questions About Date Reported </h2>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What's the difference between date of loss and date reported?</b> </p>
<p>The date of loss refers to when an incident or loss actually occurs, while the date reported is when that loss or claim is first reported to the insurance company. In a claims-made policy or a claims-made and reported policy, both dates are important to determine whether a claim is covered. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>Does late reporting of a claim always lead to a claim denial?</b> </p>
<p>Not always, but it can significantly increase the risk of a claim denial, particularly under a claims-made or claims-made and reported policy. Late reporting might also violate your policy conditions, potentially leading to a loss of coverage. </p>
<p aria-level="3"><b>What impact does the date reported have on an occurrence basis policy?</b> </p>
<p>In an occurrence basis policy, the date reported has typically less impact. These policies provide coverage for claims arising from incidents during the policy period, regardless of when they are reported. </p>

<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Date Reported </h2>
<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A small business owner noticed a minor water damage in their office but didn't report it to their carrier until months later when it worsened. The insurer denied the claim as it was a claims-made and reported policy, and the report was outside the policy period. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 2: </b>A professional liability claim was made against a consultant for advice given three years prior. Though she had a claims-made policy in place at the time of the incident, her report came after the policy expired, and she hadn't purchased extended reporting period coverage, resulting in the claim denial. </p>
<p><b>Scenario 3: </b>An employment practices liability claim was reported promptly by a company under its claims-made policy. Despite the incident occurring before the policy period, the claim was covered because it was reported within the current policy period. </p>

<h2>How to Explain Date Reported to Clients </h2>
<p><b>Personal Lines client </b>Think of your policy as a window of time. Any incidents you want covered, you need to report while that window is open, or in some cases, shortly after. </p>
<p><b>Small Business owner </b>Your insurance policy is time-sensitive. If an incident occurs, report it right away, during your policy's active dates. Wait too long, and you might lose your coverage for that incident. </p>
<p><b>CFO or Risk Manager </b>Promptly reporting all claims and potential claims during your policy period is crucial to maximize coverage, especially in claims-made policies. Your date reported can affect everything from deductibles to coinsurance values. </p>
