<h2>Intentional Tort - Deliberate Acts Causing Harm </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> An intentional tort happens when one person harms another on purpose, not by accident. It's like hitting someone in a baseball game: you did it deliberately, not by mistake. <br /></p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> An intentional tort is a category of torts that describes a civil wrong resulting from an intentional act on the part of the tortfeasor. The common intentional torts are battery, assault, false imprisonment, intentional infliction of emotional distress, conversion, and defamation. Intentional torts are typically covered under tort law. </p><p>Imagine being a baseball player who intentionally hits another player with a pitch to harm them, not by accident. Now, visualize being held accountable in a court for that deliberate act. This is the crux of an intentional tort. </p>

					<h2>What Is an Intentional Tort in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>In the realm of insurance, intentional torts are significant. They typically refer to actions causing harm or damage done deliberately rather than accidently. Intentional torts often fall under civil lawsuits where liability is assessed based on the wrongful acts committed. In contrast to other types of torts that may involve negligence or reckless behavior, such as a car accident due to careless driving, an intentional tort stems from a deliberate act. <br /></p><p>Agencies often encounter intentional torts in liability policies, especially in the context of exclusions. Since general liability policies are designed to cover accidents, not deliberate harm or damage, intentional torts are frequently excluded. </p><p>Understanding intentional torts allows insurance professionals to better anticipate and handle potential liability exposures. It's also a key element in risk management approach, enabling better advisory for personal and business clients. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Intentional Tort </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What distinguishes an intentional tort from other types of torts?</b> </p><p>An intentional tort is distinct from other types of torts like negligent or strict liability torts because it involves a deliberate act resulting in harm. Proximate cause isn't enough in such cases; there must be an intentional act. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Are intentional torts covered by insurance?</b> </p><p>Generally, intentional torts are excluded from general liability and other insurance policies because insurers don't cover deliberate acts intended to cause harm. However, it's crucial to note this can vary based on the specific policy and carrier. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How do intentional torts affect liability in a personal injury case?</b> </p><p>In a personal injury case, an intentional tort can result in liability for the perpetrator and potentially punitive damages, in addition to compensatory damages. Emotional distress, an intentional tort claim, may be associated with personal injury cases. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>What are common intentional tort case examples?</b> </p><p>Common intentional tort cases include defamation suits, incidents involving false imprisonment, and cases of invasion of privacy. These tort litigation cases rely on a conscious and deliberate violation of personal rights. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Intentional Tort </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A client owns a bar where a fight breaks out, and one patron intentionally harms another. The wounded party sues the client, citing the deliberate act. The client's general liability policy doesn't cover intentional harm, creating a coverage gap. <br /></p><p><b>Scenario 2: </b>An business professional sues her rival for defamation, a type of intentional tort. The rival made false statements damaging her reputation. She might seek compensatory and punitive damages. In such cases, the defendant's insurance might not offer coverage due to the intentional nature of the act. </p><p><b>Scenario 3: </b>A celebrity sues a publication for intentional infliction of emotional distress following a series of invasive articles. As emotional distress is an intentional tort, the magazine's general liability insurance might not cover the lawsuit, leaving the publication exposed. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Intentional Tort to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Personal Lines client </b>"Think of intentional torts as purposely throwing a baseball and hitting a window. Your homeowner's policy may not cover damage from intentional acts, like vandalism, but it's there to protect you in case the kids hit a stray ball into the neighbor's window by accident." <br /></p><p><b>CFO or Risk Manager </b>"In terms of your company's liability risk, intentional torts are deliberate actions that result in harm, like defamation or false imprisonment. These aren't typically covered by general liability policies, so it's essential that your company has risk management strategies that include avoiding such actions." </p><p><b>Small Business owner </b>"Picture getting sued because an employee intentionally damaged a customer's goods. This is an intentional tort, and insurance usually doesn't cover these deliberate actions. It's important to train your staff to act responsibly and understand the potential consequences of such behavior." </p>
