<h2>Strict Liability – Responsibility Without Negligence </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> Strict liability is a legal rule that holds someone responsible for the harm caused by their actions, regardless of whether they were careful or negligent. It's like being held accountable for breaking a window while playing ball, even if you didn't intend to do it. </p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> In insurance and legal contexts, strict liability refers to the legal responsibility of a party for damages or harm caused by their activity, irrespective of fault or intent. This accountability is most commonly associated with product liability cases, certain hazardous activities, and the possession of dangerous animals. In the pertinent insurance policy forms, the strict liability provision applies as a determining factor during claims evaluation and litigation. </p><p>In the world of insurance and law, unintentional harm doesn't always spare you from legal responsibility. The principle of strict liability means you can be held responsible for a client's harm or loss, even if you did your best to prevent it.</p>

					<h2>What Is Strict Liability in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>The principle of strict liability inflicts legal responsibility for injury on the party in the best position to prevent the harm. It's typically invoked in three scenarios: when abnormally dangerous or hazardous activities are involved, in product liability cases, and when injuries are caused by wild or exotic animals. </p><p>Strict liability is significant in tort law and criminal law, and is a fundamental part of insurance policies where it impacts coverage and claims processing. Also, a standard underlying concept in strict liability is the "inherently dangerous" nature of an activity or product, which means it poses an abnormally high risk. </p><p>Agencies should be aware of the potential implications of strict liability on clients operating in risky sectors like manufacturing, refineries, oil wells, or those dealing with explosive materials, toxic chemicals, rocket motors, wild or exotic animals, etc. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Strict Liability </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>What categorizes as an abnormally dangerous activity?</b> </p><p>This often varies by state and the law but generally includes activities that inherently pose a significant risk to others or property. Examples include crop dusting, pile driving, hazardous waste disposal, manufacturing of flammable liquids, etc. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Why is strict liability significant in product liability?</b> </p><p>Strict liability applies in product liability cases because it protects consumers from harm caused by defective products. Suppose a consumer gets injured because of a product defect. In that case, the manufacturer can be held responsible, irrespective of the quality controls they had in place or whether they were unaware of the defect. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How does strict liability differ from negligence?</b> </p><p>With negligence, the harmed party must prove the wrongdoer didn't uphold a reasonable standard of care. However, in strict liability cases, courts only require proof that the defendant's action caused harm, making establishing fault easier. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How does strict liability apply to exotic animals?</b> </p><p>Strict liability often applies to injuries caused by animals known to be dangerous or not domesticated, like owning a pet tiger. If the animal causes harm, the owner can be held strictly liable irrespective of any precautions taken to prevent the harm. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Strict Liability </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1:</b> A client owned an aircraft manufacturing company. A private small aircraft crashed due to a faulty engine, leading to fatalities. Even though robust quality controls were in place and the engine supplier was entrusted with providing safe engines, the strict liability law held the aircraft manufacturer responsible for the tragedy. </p><p><b>Scenario 2:</b> A client operates an exotic animal sanctuary. Despite stringent safety measures and warning signs, a visitor was attacked by a leopard during a tour. The sanctuary was held under strict liability for the injuries due to the inherent risk associated with exotic animals. </p><p><b>Scenario 3:</b> A client running a pesticide production unit suffered strict liability claims after a batch of their product resulted in substantial crop damage for farmers. Even though there had been no prior issues with their product, the strict liability attached to their product put the responsibility for compensation on them. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Strict Liability to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Personal Lines client </b>Suppose you had a dog, and it bit someone. Even if you had taken all necessary precautions, you could be held responsible for damages because of strict liability. </p><p><b>Small Business owner </b>Imagine if a product you put out accidentally harms a customer. With strict liability, you could still be legally responsible for damages, even if you wasn’t aware of the defect in the product. </p><p><b>CFO or Risk Manager </b>Consider an instance where your company performs abnormally hazardous activities, like handling toxic chemicals. If an accident occurs despite all precautions, your company can be held responsible under strict liability and could face significant financial loss.</p>
