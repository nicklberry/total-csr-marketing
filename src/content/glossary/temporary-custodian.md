<h2>Temporary Custodian – A Covered Role In Crime Policy </h2>				
		<p><b>In plain language:</b> A temporary custodian is someone legally responsible for an item's security and care on a short-term basis. In essence, this person is a temporary "guard" for a possession. <br /></p><p><b>Technical definition:</b> In insurance, particularly in a Crime Policy, a temporary custodian is an individual or entity who holds, has control of, or is in charge of any insured property for a brief, defined period. This term can be found in policy conditions that set forth who is covered. In the context of theft, disappearance, or destruction coverage, a temporary custodian might include a bailee or a repair service, for example. </p><p>Imagine a jeweler temporarily holding a client’s expensive watch for repair. The watch is stolen while in the jeweler's possession. Who pays for the loss? Would it fall under the jeweler’s crime policy? Understanding the concept of a "temporary custodian" can significantly impact coverage situations. </p>

					<h2>What Is a Temporary Custodian in Insurance? </h2>				
		<p>A temporary custodian refers to a role acknowledged in insurance policies where a person or organization has been entrusted temporarily with someone else's property. This term is prevalently seen in Crime Policies where protection is provided to the business against loss of money, securities, or inventory resulting from crime. <br /></p><p>Typically, the term's existence within a policy form can be found in defined terms or conditions. Its presence is identical to a key that unlocks a particular coverage or exclusion. Though the majority of discussions surrounding this term occur in the context of Crime Policies, one might find it in other policy forms as well. </p><p>One important factor that agencies must recognize is that not everyone in temporary control or possession of insured property is considered a temporary custodian under the policy. This differentiation comes down to the policy's wording, the temporary custodian’s legal duty, and how these align with the underlying loss scenario. </p>

					<h2>Common Questions About Temporary Custodian </h2>				
		<p aria-level="3"><b>How does being a Temporary Custodian affect claim outcomes?</b> </p><p>A temporary custodian's role is crucial during claims processing when the insured property under temporary custody sustains loss or damage. Depending upon the policy wording, a claim can either be accepted or denied based on whether the individual or business holding the property is deemed a temporary custodian. </p><p>For example, if a diamond ring is stolen from a jeweler entrusted with the ring for resizing, assuming the jeweler is acknowledged as a temporary custodian in the owner's Crime Policy, the resultant theft claim may be accepted under that policy. If the jeweler is not recognized as a temporary custodian, however, the claim could be denied. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>Who qualifies as a Temporary Custodian?</b> </p><p>The policy will outline who qualifies as a temporary custodian. Typically, it is someone who has legal responsibility for someone else's property for a short period, such as a repairman or a parking valet. </p><p aria-level="3"><b>How can agencies reduce E&O exposure related to Temporary Custodian?</b> </p><p>Agencies can reduce E&O (Errors and Omissions) exposures by clearly defining the term "Temporary Custodian" to clients, especially those who provide bailee services. <br /></p><p>Agencies also need to make sure adequate coverage is in place should a temporary custodian be involved in a loss scenario. It’s also crucial that agencies document all communications made to clients about this term and its implications on their coverage. </p>

					<h2>Real Claim Examples Involving Temporary Custodian </h2>				
		<p><b>Scenario 1: </b>A client left their luxury car to be serviced at the local garage. The car was stolen overnight from the garage. Helped by their agent, the client claimed under their Auto Policy believing the garage to be a temporary custodian. The claim was denied as the policy did not recognize the garage as a temporary custodian. <br /></p><p><b>Scenario 2: </b>A jeweller lost stones they had taken in for resetting. Their Business Owner's Policy provided limited coverage as the policy did not consider the jeweller as a temporary custodian of the stones. The jeweler was advised to review their coverage and consider a Bailee's Customer Insurance policy, which provides broader coverage. </p><p><b>Scenario 3: </b>A courier company lost a package containing expensive electronics. The claim made by the sender under its Crime Policy was honored as the courier company was considered a temporary custodian for the package. </p>

					<h2>How to Explain Temporary Custodian to Clients </h2>				
		<p><b>Car Owner: </b>"Think of the term 'Temporary Custodian' like a valet you hand your car keys to. For a short time, they are in charge of your car. In insurance, we use this term to identify who, besides yourself, can be responsible for your property." <br /></p><p><b>Jeweler: </b>"<i>Temporary Custodian</i> is when, like in your case as a jeweler, a customer's property is in your care for a certain period, say, during resizing or repair.
