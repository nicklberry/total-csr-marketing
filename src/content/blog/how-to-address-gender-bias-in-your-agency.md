<p>The insurance industry has struggled with issues surrounding sexism for its entire existence.&nbsp; Insurance is a very male-dominated industry, with most leadership positions still held by men.&nbsp; Many agencies have operated with very traditional gender-specific roles within their organizations, while others have been more progressive.</p>
<p>Despite the significant improvement over recent years, there is still work to create better gender equality.&nbsp; Here are a few critical tips agencies can utilize to move the needle forward:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ol type="1">
<li>
<h2><strong>Address Issues Within the Agency –</strong><strong><br></strong></h2>
</li>
</ol>
</li>
</ol>
<p>In some agencies, the discriminatory conversation is so typical that it has become normalized to most staff members.&nbsp; Women assume it is acceptable correspondence, and men in the agency feel it is okay to continue speaking the way they always have about colleagues of the opposite sex.</p>
<p>As common as this type of commentary may be in your agency, it is critical to address it once you notice it.&nbsp; Failure to tackle these types of conversations is ignoring harassing behavior.&nbsp; It is damaging to the agency's women and harmful to your reputation with clients and future employees.&nbsp; Instead, making a concerted effort to address unacceptable communication is the best way to rid your agency of it.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>
<p>To do this, agencies should:</p>
<ul>
<li>- Develop a written policy regarding sexual harassment and discrimination and make it part of the employee handbook and conduct.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li>- Address organizational issues and changes with the entire agency.&nbsp; Ensure employees understand expectations and consequences.</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li>- Address specific problems directly with the individuals involved.&nbsp; Again, clarifying expectations for what needs to change and outlining consequences if the behavior does not improve.&nbsp;</li><li><br></li>
<li>
<h2><strong>Review and Revise Agency Structure</strong><strong><br></strong></h2>
</li>
</ul>
<p>While sexism issues are not exclusive to men versus women, it is more commonplace in arrangements where men and women work one on one.&nbsp; In other words, when one account manager handling all a producer’s book, it can lead to an unbalanced dynamic with the producer asserting an upper hand to the account manager.&nbsp;</p>
<p>To reduce issues the agency is currently experiencing and prevent future problems, shift accounts around.&nbsp; Have producers working with a variety of account managers.&nbsp; Also, ensure that reporting lines are clear.&nbsp; It is understandable for a producer to request and expect account managers to meet clients' service needs, which gives the producer carte blanche.&nbsp; Any special requests outside of standard workflows or agency protocol should require a supervisor's approval rather than the&nbsp;producer's permission.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<h2><strong>Advocate for Women in Your Agency</strong><strong><br></strong></h2>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Communication is first and foremost to shine a spotlight on gender inequality issues in your agency and the insurance industry.&nbsp; Create a platform that fosters open dialogue to discuss problems females in your agency are experiencing in their work.</p>
<p>To ensure efforts, maintain momentum, recruit, train, and promote females into your agency's leadership positions.&nbsp; Provide opportunities for women in the agency to join the local community's leadership council geared towards women in business.</p>
<p>Creating better gender equality in insurance requires support from both men and women.&nbsp; Starting with the tips above can help your agency make great strides and elevate the conversation across the entire industry.</p>
