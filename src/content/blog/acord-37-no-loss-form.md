<h2>Understanding the Purpose of the ACORD No Loss Form</h2>				
															<img width="800" height="340" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACORD-37-1024x435.jpg" alt="Graphic of ACORD 37, also known as the Statement of No Loss form" srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACORD-37-1024x435.jpg 1024w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACORD-37-300x128.jpg 300w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACORD-37-768x326.jpg 768w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ACORD-37.jpg 1254w" sizes="(max-width: 800px) 100vw, 800px" />															
		<p>The ACORD 37 form or statement of no loss form, is a crucial insurance document used primarily to verify that an insured party hasn't experienced any losses or claims during a lapse or gap in insurance coverage. This property insurance no loss statement helps insurance carriers confidently renew or reinstate a policy without concerns about hidden or undisclosed losses.</p><p><strong><a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-agency-blog/the-role-of-an-insurance-account-manager-responsibilities-skills-and-career-insights/">Insurance professionals</a></strong> regularly encounter situations where a client's policy renewal date passes or coverage lapses due to nonpayment, administrative oversights, or unforeseen issues. Utilizing the ACORD No Known Loss letter safeguards insurers from unknowingly assuming liability for events occurring during periods without coverage. In this guide, we'll walk you step by step through accurately completing the form, provide advice on common pitfalls, and show how Total CSR's software can also help accelerate your team’s operations and processes moving forward.</p>		
					<h2>When to Use an ACORD Statement of  No Loss Form</h2>

		<p>Typical situations for using a no loss letter include:</p><ul><li>Policy reinstatement following cancellation</li><li>Renewal policies processed after the original expiration date</li><li>Delayed processing due to <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/underwriters/">underwriting </a>or payment-related issues</li></ul>		
					<h2>Step-by-Step Guide to Completing the ACORD 37 - No Loss Form</h2>				
					<h3><b>Step 1: Verify Policyholder Information</b></h3>				
		<p>Begin by verifying:</p><ul><li>Named insured</li><li>Policy number</li><li>Effective dates of the policy gap or lapse</li></ul><p>Ensure these details exactly match existing insurance policy documentation to prevent discrepancies.</p>		
					<h3><b>Step 2: Clarify Coverage Gap Period</b></h3>				
		<p>Clearly indicate the period when coverage lapsed. Document precise dates (day, month, year), including the cancellation date and the reinstatement effective date. Note that many policies start at 12:01 AM on the effective date, which should be clearly stated if relevant.</p>		
					<h3><b>Step 3: Affirm No Losses or Claims</b></h3>				
		<p>The insured must explicitly confirm that no losses, claims, or incidents occurred during the uninsured interval. It's crucial that policyholders fully understand the significance of their declaration, as inaccuracies may lead to future claim denials or allegations of insurance fraud. The statement must clearly indicate there were no accidents or circumstances that might result in a claim.</p>		
					<h3><b>Step 4: Insured's Signature</b></h3>				
		<p>Secure a handwritten or digitally authenticated applicant's signature, ensuring the date of signature is included. Certain forms might also require a witness signature or producer certification for further verification.</p>

					<h2>Common Acord 37 Statement of No Loss Mistakes and How to Avoid Them</h2>				
					<h3>Ambiguity About Dates</h3>				
		<p>Accurate documentation of dates is vital. Ambiguity regarding lapse periods can cause underwriting delays or claim disputes. Always cross-check dates with policy records, including effective and expiration dates.</p>		
					<h3>Overlooking the Signature</h3>				
		<p>An unsigned statement of no loss form is invalid. Always confirm that the insured has properly signed and dated the document prior to submission for proper policy verification.</p>		
					<h3>Incorrect Policy Information</h3>				
		<p>Double-check policy numbers, NAIC codes, and named insured details against your agency management system for consistency, preventing errors and additional work. The NAIC code is particularly crucial for accurately identifying the insurance carrier involved.</p>		
					<h3>Real-World Scenario</h3>				
		<p>Consider a construction firm whose General Liability insurance policy renewal was inadvertently overlooked, resulting in a five-day gap without coverage. Before reinstating the policy, the carrier requires an ACORD statement of no loss. Accurately completing this liability insurance no loss form is vital for quickly restoring coverage and ensuring no hidden claims occurred during this gap in liability coverage.</p>		
					<h2>Best Practices for Insurance Professionals on the Statement of - No Loss Form (ACORD 37)</h2>				
		<ul><li>Clearly educate insured parties about the purpose and implications of signing the insurance no loss declaration.</li><li>Keep thorough documentation, securely storing ACORD forms electronically.</li><li>Utilize standardized checklists to verify information before submitting to <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/underwriters/">underwriters</a>.</li><li>Ensure all contact information and the agency customer ID are current.</li><li>As a producer, maintain clear communication with both the insured and the insurance company throughout the entire process.</li></ul>

					<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>				
												<a tabindex="0">What is a statement of no loss?</a>
					<p>A statement of no loss is a form (commonly ACORD 37) that verifies no claims occurred during a coverage lapse. It’s essential for policy reinstatement and helps insurance carriers assess risk when resuming coverage after a gap.</p>
												<a tabindex="0">What happens if a loss did occur during the lapse period?</a>
					<p>If any claims or incidents occurred during the lapse period, the insurer may deny reinstatement or choose to re-underwrite the policy.  The ACORD 37 should not be signed if a loss has occurred, since doing so could be considered fraud.</p>
												<a tabindex="0">How should insurance agencies handle ACORD 37 compliance?</a>
					<p><b>Agencies should:</b></p><ul><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1">Ensure clients understand what they are signing</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1">Document timelines carefully when dealing with reinstatements</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1">Use agency management systems (AMS) to track and store signed forms</li><li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1">Avoid backdating or applying pressure to sign under unclear conditions</li></ul>
