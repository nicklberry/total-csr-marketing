<h2>What Is an ACORD 130 Form?​</h2>				
															<img width="800" height="340" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Acord-130-Logo.jpg" alt="" srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Acord-130-Logo.jpg 1024w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Acord-130-Logo-300x127.jpg 300w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Acord-130-Logo-768x326.jpg 768w" sizes="(max-width: 800px) 100vw, 800px" />															
		<p>If you're beginning a career in insurance, it's important to learn about the applications like the ACORD 130 used in the Agency Management System (AMS).</p><p>ACORD forms, created by the ACORD Corporation, are standardized documents recognized across the insurance industry. They include all the details insurers require to issue quotes. Different ACORD forms exist for specific coverage types, and a common one you'll encounter is the ACORD 130</p><p>The ACORD 130 form, more widely known as the Workers Compensation Application, is used to gather <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/policy/">policy </a>data needed to rate workers compensation. This workers compensation insurance application includes critical information like remuneration (payroll), loss history, and other operational details.</p><p>You might wonder how to fill out ACORD 130. In short, it's essential, because this workers comp application is needed for every workers compensation policy. Some insurance professionals refer to it as the ACORD application for brevity. Accuracy on this form determines not only the efficiency of processing but also the eligibility for workers compensation insurance.</p><p>Let's explore the main areas to focus on when completing an ACORD 130.</p>

					<h2>How to Complete the ACORD 130 Form Step-by-Step</h2>				
					<h3>Page 1: Core Details  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p><strong>Agency and Applicant Information</strong></p><p>Start by entering your agency information. If you're using an AMS, it may auto-populate. Otherwise, input the required data manually. Next, fill in the Applicant Information on the right side of the page:</p><ul><li><b>Applicant name</b></li><li><b>Phone number(s)</b></li><li><b>Years in Business</b></li><li><b>Mailing address</b></li><li><b>Applicant Entity Type</b></li><li><b>ID Number (if applicable)</b></li><li><b>Federal Employer ID Number (EIN)</b></li></ul>		
															<img width="624" height="223" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Workers-Compensation-Application.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation application capturing agency details, applicant entity type, years in business, and federal employer ID information." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Workers-Compensation-Application.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Workers-Compensation-Application-300x107.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Submission and Billing Details  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p>Below that, choose the Status of Submission—for new quotes, select "quote." Under Billing/Audit Information, note who gets billed (the insured or the agency) and identify the chosen <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/premium/">premium </a>payment plan. There's also a space to indicate the policy's audit frequency.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="67" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Submission-and-Billing.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation submission and billing section showing status (quote, bound, assigned risk), billing plan (agency or direct), payment plan, and audit frequency." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Submission-and-Billing.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Submission-and-Billing-300x32.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
		<p>The next area you will find in the ACORD 130 is the Locations section. Here you will need to enter the location number and full address for the insured. Use additional lines as needed to record all of the insured's locations, including any work from home arrangements.</p>		
					<h3>Locations Section </h3>				
		<p>List each insured location, including any work from home setups. If there are multiple sites, add them on additional lines.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="105" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Locations.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation locations section listing multiple insured sites, including addresses and highest floors for each workplace." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Locations.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Locations-300x50.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Policy Information  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p>Use this section to record:</p><ul><li>Effective and expiration dates</li><li>State(s) where the insured operates (including any ACORD 130 FL specific information for Florida)</li><li>Employer's liability limits</li><li>Other states the insured might need coverage in</li></ul>		
					<h3>Contact Information  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p>If you've completed the <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-agency-blog/how-to-complete-the-acord-125">ACORD 125</a> for the same client, you can skip this area. If not, enter the applicant's contact details, ensuring all information is accurate.</p>		
					<h3>Individuals Included/Excluded </h3>				
		<p>Here, identify owners, partners, officers, or relatives and indicate whether they're included or excluded from workers compensation benefits. Supply details such as state, location number, title, ownership percentage, and remuneration.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="148" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Individuals-Included-Excluded.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation form section listing owners, partners, officers, or relatives, with details on inclusion/exclusion, ownership percentage, and remuneration." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Individuals-Included-Excluded.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Individuals-Included-Excluded-300x71.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>State Rating Worksheet & Premium Information - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p>On Page 2, you'll find the State Rating Worksheet, where you detail each location's specific job duties. Include:</p><ul><li><b>Class code</b></li><li><b>Categories</b></li><li><b>Duties and classifications</b></li><li><b>Number of full-time and part-time employees</b></li></ul><p>If payroll is broken down by various tasks, list each task separately with the appropriate class codes. This can lower the minimum premium if done correctly.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="469" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-State-Rating-Worksheet.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation state rating worksheet capturing class codes, duties, number of full-time and part-time employees, and payroll breakdowns for each location." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-State-Rating-Worksheet.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-State-Rating-Worksheet-300x225.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Premium Section  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p>Focus on the Experience Mod, assigned by NCCI (National Council on Compensation Insurance) based on loss history. An insured with three years of workers' comp experience and a premium over $3,000 might have an experience modification. A modifier under 1.0 lowers costs; one above 1.0 raises premiums.</p><p>Other items here include:</p><ul><li><b>Premium discount</b></li><li><b>Deposit premium</b></li><li><b>Any consideration for assigned risk</b></li><li><b>Rating information</b></li></ul>		
															<img width="624" height="170" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Premium-Section.png" alt="ACORD 130 workers compensation premium section featuring experience modification, assigned risk factors, premium discounts, deposit premiums, and overall rating information." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Premium-Section.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Premium-Section-300x82.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Policy History & Business Operations  - ACORD 130</h3>				
		<p><strong>Prior Carrier & Loss History</strong></p><p>Document the last five years of coverage, including prior carrier information:</p><ul><li><b>Policy year</b></li><li><b>Carrier name</b></li><li><b>Policy number</b></li><li><b>Premium</b></li><li><b>Experience Mod (if any)</b></li><li><b>Claim counts and claim amounts (paid or reserved</b>)</li></ul><p>This is key for NCCI when calculating the experience modification factor.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="176" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Prior-Carrier-Loss-History.png" alt="workers compensation form listing prior carrier details, policy years, premiums, experience modifications, and claims history for the last five years." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Prior-Carrier-Loss-History.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Prior-Carrier-Loss-History-300x85.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Nature of Business </h3>				
		<p>Use this area to describe the insured's operations, including any subcontractors or leased employees. If you're not submitting <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-agency-blog/how-to-complete-the-acord-125"><b><u>ACORD 125</u></b></a>, you must fill this out.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="169" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Nature-of-Business.png" alt="workers compensation form section describing the insured’s operations, including any subcontractors, leased employees, and manufacturing or service details." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Nature-of-Business.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-Nature-of-Business-300x81.png 300w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>General Information Section</h3>				
		<p>Spread across Page 3 and Page 4, this part has 24 yes/no questions covering:</p><ul><li>Safety programs</li><li>Seasonal employees</li><li>Physical handicaps of employees</li><li>Whether coverage declined in the past</li><li>Bankruptcy and tax liens</li></ul><p>Any "yes" requires an explanation. Pay special attention to questions about safety program implementation, as this can affect premiums.</p>		
															<img width="624" height="760" src="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-General-Information.png" alt="general information section spanning pages 3 and 4, featuring 24 yes/no questions about safety programs, seasonal employees, prior coverage declines, and other underwriting details." srcset="https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-General-Information.png 624w, https://totalcsr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/ACORD-130-General-Information-246x300.png 246w" sizes="(max-width: 624px) 100vw, 624px" />															
					<h3>Finalizing the Form</h3>				
		<p>At the bottom, you'll see signature boxes for both the <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/producer/"><b><u>producer </u></b></a>and insured. While these aren't required for a quote, they are necessary to bind the <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/policy/"><b><u>policy</u></b></a>.</p>		
					<h3>Additional Notes</h3>				
		<ul><li><b>A fillable ACORD 130 form is generally found in your AMS.</b></li><li><b>You can send an ACORD form PDF to the client for review and signature.</b></li><li><b>Some data auto-fills in the AMS, saving time.</b></li></ul><p>The ACORD 130 is critical for issuing workers compensation insurance. It allows carriers to review policy information, loss history, and other risk factors to price coverage appropriately. By filling it out accurately, you ensure clients get the coverage they need.</p><p>Remember to consider the NCCI risk ID for experience rating when working with ACORD 130.</p><p>Within the form, you'll see sections for rating information and schedule rating, both of which help determine the estimated annual <a href="https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/premium/"><b><u>premium</u></b></a>. Accurately completing them ensures the premium is calculated properly.</p><p>Stay informed of updates to the ACORD 130 form, as industry requirements evolve. Always use the most recent version of the ACORD 130 to remain compliant.</p>

					<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>				
												<a tabindex="0">Is the duty to defend broader than the duty to indemnify?</a>
					<p>Yes, the duty to defend is broader than the duty to indemnify because it applies to potential coverage, not just actual coverage</p>
												<a tabindex="0">When is the duty to defend triggered?</a>
					<p>The duty to defend is triggered when the insurer receives notice of a lawsuit that might potentially result in covered liability</p>
												<a tabindex="0">Does an insurer have to defend against groundless claims?</a>
					<p>Yes, an insurer must defend against any lawsuit that might potentially result in a covered judgment, even if the claim is groundless, false, or fraudulent</p>
												<a tabindex="0">What happens if an insurer breaches its duty to defend?</a>
					<p>If an insurer breaches its duty to defend, it may face a breach of contract or bad faith complaint from the policyholder</p>
												<a tabindex="0">How does the duty to defend differ in various insurance policies?</a>
					<p>The duty to defend can vary between policies, with some giving the insurer control over the defense and others allowing the insured to manage their own defense</p>
